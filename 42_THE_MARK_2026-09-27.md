# The mark: the Folded A (27 Sep 2026)

**Eric's choice:** "I like the Folded A design that you came up with, including the dot. That is what we will use for the iOS/Android/Windows application icon." Then: "I like the rounded edges of the icon," and, of the three launcher shapes, "the middle option": the rounded square.

## What it is

One strip of paper, folded once. It rises in ink, turns over at a flat crease, and comes down showing its orange back, the way origami paper is coloured on one side. Where it lies over itself, the paper darkens: the fold. The dot is the one thing that matters today, and it's the part that moves.

It replaces the dot over an arc, which Eric said read as Amazon and Headspace combined, and the hanging hairline trace before it.

## How it got here

1. Superdesign generated four directions. None landed.
2. I drew about 18 of my own and rendered each at 440, 64, 32 and 16 px, in the tray and in three colourways. *Meridian* was sent.
3. Eric preferred an early one, the Folded A.
4. The Folded A was then worked through the design plugins, each for what it's for:

| plugin | what it changed |
|---|---|
| design critique | The apex became a flat crease, where it had been a point. The dot drops the fold tone below 32 px instead of merging into the legs. The dot is the one moving part. |
| brand review | Atlassian's old mark is also a two-piece A. The flat crease, a single strip and the visible back face make ours paper, not two shapes. (A design judgement, not a trademark search.) |
| colour palette | One accent. The fold is shown as a tonal step, not a shadow. Dark themes use contrast, not glow. |
| contrast (WCAG graphics, 3:1) | Every part passes on its tile. The orange-tile version failed at the fold (2.2:1), so the icon's tile is Warm Paper cream. |
| motion | Every state is built from 150, 200 and 300 ms steps, easing out. Only position, size and opacity move. With Reduce Motion on, it stays still. |

5. **Found at close-up:** the two strips crossed above the apex like an X, which paper can't do. The apex now sits *on* the crease, so the strips meet there, and a test holds that rule.

## One source, everywhere

`src/mark.rs` holds the shapes, the colours and the motion (`pose`). Everything else comes from it:

| where | what |
|---|---|
| Atlas's own windows | `window::paint_mark`, which draws the setup window, the panels, the **morning brief** (waking) and the **idle overlay**, plus every window's icon (`mark::window_icon`) |
| the hub | `hub::MARK`, coloured by the page's own tokens; a test holds it to `mark::for_the_hub()` |
| Windows | `assets/atlas.ico` (16 to 256 px, rounded tile, with the dot at every size), linked into `atlas.exe` by `build.rs` from `windows/atlas.res` (MSVC) or `atlas-res.o` (GNU), with the name **Atlas** and its version for Task Manager and Windows' prompts |
| iPhone and iPad | `Assets.xcassets/AppIcon`: light, dark and tinted, 1024 px; iOS rounds it |
| Android | an adaptive icon that is **always the rounded square**: the tile is drawn in the icon itself over a clear background, so a circle or squircle launcher mask can't change it. Plus a monochrome layer for themed icons, and the notification icon, both replacing the system's placeholder icons |
| the web app | rounded icons at 192 and 512; a square maskable one; the Apple touch icon |

`design/mark/make_marks.py` writes every file from `mark.rs`'s own numbers, and `tests/the_mark_is_one_mark.rs` fails if any file drifts.

## It follows each person's appearance

Eric: "I want the first 3's colour option dependent on how the user has their settings, and the 5th's rounded edges."

| where | light | dark | tinted | who decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone and iPad | Warm Paper tile | Ember tile, the mark in its dark colours | a grey mark on black, which iOS tints | iOS, from the person's Home Screen setting; iOS rounds every one |
| Android | Warm Paper rounded square | Ember rounded square (`drawable-night`) | the monochrome layer, for themed icons | Android, from the phone's dark theme and themed icons settings |
| Atlas's own windows and taskbar | `mark-256.png` | `mark-256-dark.png` | — | Atlas, from its own appearance (which follows Windows by default); it swaps the icon the moment that changes |

Every one of them is the rounded tile.

**What can't follow:** the icon on `atlas.exe` itself, in File Explorer and on a pinned shortcut. Windows gives a program one icon, so that one stays light. The web app's manifest icons are fixed too.

## Motion

| state | what moves |
|---|---|
| Idle | dim, a 4.8 s breath (60–85% opacity) |
| Thinking | the dot rises 26 units toward the crease and settles, every 1.2 s; the fold lightens as it rises |
| Speaking | the dot follows Atlas's real voice level; the folded leg answers it |
| Waking (morning brief) | ink leg rises (0–300 ms), the strip folds over (300–600), the crease darkens (600–750), the dot arrives (750–950) |

## Measured

- Personal Atlas: **34 targets, 7,056 passed, 0 failed**. The first run had 6 failures, all from this change, all fixed:
  - the module count;
  - two functions only tests called;
  - a test helper placed where the dead-code counter's parser didn't expect it;
  - the PNG check assuming every icon has an alpha channel;
  - the catalogue document.
- Rendered and looked at: the Windows `.ico` at every size, the iPhone light, dark and tinted icons, the web icons, and Android's icon under circle, rounded and squircle masks.
